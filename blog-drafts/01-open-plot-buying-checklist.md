# Open Plot Buying Checklist: What to Review Before You Pay

**Slug:** open-plot-buying-checklist  
**Category:** Buying Guide  
**Excerpt:** A practical checklist for reviewing a plotted property in and around Hyderabad before paying an advance or signing an agreement.

Buying an open plot can be an important long-term decision. A location may look promising, but buyers should review the paperwork, layout, access and payment terms before committing money. Use this checklist as a starting point and verify every project-specific detail independently.

## 1. Confirm who owns the land

Ask for the current title documents and trace how ownership moved to the present seller. Names, survey numbers, extents and boundaries should be consistent across the documents. If there are multiple owners, inherited interests or powers of attorney, ask a property lawyer to review the authority to sell.

## 2. Verify the layout approval

Ask which authority approved the layout and request the approval proceedings and sanctioned layout plan. Check that the plot number, dimensions, roads, open spaces and survey details match the approved plan. Do not rely only on a brochure, a marketing map or a verbal assurance.

## 3. Check the land records and encumbrances

Obtain the relevant current land-record extracts and an Encumbrance Certificate for an appropriate period. These checks can help identify recorded transactions or interests, but they are not a substitute for a complete title investigation. Ask a lawyer to review the records and explain any gaps or entries.

## 4. Visit the site

Visit the actual survey location. Compare the plot boundaries and access roads with the approved plan. Look at drainage, surrounding development, electricity access and road conditions. If possible, visit at different times and speak with nearby residents.

## 5. Read the agreement and payment schedule

Make sure the agreement identifies the seller, property description, plot number, total price, taxes and charges, payment milestones, possession terms and refund or default provisions. Get a written receipt for every payment and avoid paying into an unrelated personal account.

## 6. Get independent advice

Before paying a substantial advance, have an independent property lawyer review the title chain, approvals, land records and draft agreement. Confirm current requirements with the relevant authorities because rules and records can change.

## Final thought

A careful review may take time, but it helps you understand exactly what you are buying. Keep copies of the documents you receive and do not proceed while important questions remain unanswered.
